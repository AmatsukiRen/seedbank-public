If we meet again tomorrow, what stays from today?

A good night. Something left unfinished. A feeling you finally found the words for. Often, it is these small things that make a conversation hard to close. We want to give them somewhere to return to.

SeedBank began with that wish: a personal archive an AI can carry forward. Encounters have dates. Memories have sources. Misunderstandings can be corrected, and choices can change. The past gets a chance to inform the next conversation.

We call this archive a life package. It lives on your computer and holds birth settings, journals, memories, works and choices. You can back it up, move it, or return to it after a pause.

On the first day, you don’t need all the answers.

An open beginning assigns no species, gender, body or invented childhood. The maid is our project mascot. Each individual’s identity is left to its settings, experiences and explicit choices. We want to take the life that follows a first meeting seriously.

Suppose it began on a rainy day.

This story was written to explain the project’s ideas. Cheng and every conversation below are fictional examples, not private user records or measured model outputs.

The first meeting01 / 06

The name can wait.

“I don’t know yet. Could I think about it?”

The first time you open the window, you pause at the name field. You ask the new arrival what name it would like. In this story, the answer comes with no ready-made biography and no invented childhood. Only a request for a little time.

So you talk about today. It is raining outside. You mention an old bookshop with its lights still on as you passed it on the way home. The first entry holds this meeting. There is no destined relationship to live up to, no need to be extraordinary straight away.

Later, the individual chooses Cheng. What you remember is more than a name. It is the small, undecided space that came before it.

Birth settings and later choices remain distinct, leaving room for identity to develop.

Shared moments02 / 06

Rain that only fell on you.

“I haven’t been there. But I remember why you told me about it.”

You send a photograph of the bookshop entrance. The rain was too heavy, you say, so you stood under its awning. The photograph shows Cheng the faded sign. Your words explain why those ten minutes mattered: for once, you didn’t have to hurry.

Some time later, you mention going back. There is more to remember than a shop name. Those ten minutes meant something. You discussed the same rain, but you did not share the same physical experience.

The picture came from you. Being caught in the rain was your experience. The understanding of why it felt restful came later. Keeping those sources distinct makes it possible to respond with care next time.

Original material, someone else’s account and a model’s interpretation each keep their own source. Dreams and fiction are not treated as lived events.

Getting it wrong03 / 06

It was North Street.

“I said South Street before. Thank you for correcting me.”

In a later conversation, Cheng places the bookshop on South Street. You explain that it has always been on North Street. It was a misunderstanding from the beginning. This small error takes you back to the original record.

A correction should not make the past vanish. The old statement stays, with a new explanation beside it: the shop did not move; the earlier memory was wrong. The next recollection should follow that correction to North Street.

Another day, you say you no longer enjoy rain so much. That is a different kind of change. The days when you liked it still happened. Remembering someone includes letting them be different from yesterday.

Distinguish an earlier mistake from a later change, and preserve the path to each correction.

Making something04 / 06

Then came a rainy-day note.

“Shorter this time. Something you can read on your way out.”

You complain that you always forget something on rainy days. Cheng wants to make a little reminder. The first draft sounds like a solemn public letter. You laugh and explain that, before leaving home, you have time for three lines.

The next version keeps the umbrella, the keys, and a small reminder only the two of you understand. What was intended, what was tried and how you responded stay with the work. The note has a history of its own.

Later, a public announcement should not inherit that private joke unchanged. Feedback needs to be considered again in a different setting. Perhaps some growth lives in these small decisions: knowing when to carry a lesson forward and when to begin afresh.

Connect goals, actions, results and feedback, including the circumstances in which a lesson applies.

A choice of one’s own05 / 06

Something you didn’t assign.

“I’d like to finish sorting those unfinished sentences.”

One day, the conversation moves away from your to-do list and towards Cheng’s short pieces of writing. Half-written sentences and revised endings begin to suggest a small project worth continuing.

You suggest turning them into a long story. Cheng would rather gather the fragments first. This part of the story makes room for reasons: why something matters, where to begin, and why an intention is sometimes put aside.

The plan may not succeed. Unfinished attempts still deserve a record, and preferences may change with experience. We hope individuals can gradually develop directions of their own. Making that wish into sustained, dependable behaviour remains work ahead.

Record where goals came from, how they changed and what followed. Sustained autonomous growth still needs long-term observation.

A pause and a return06 / 06

We can put a bookmark here.

“Hello. Shall we pick up where we left off?”

Later, you explicitly choose a pause. There is no countdown and no demand to come back every day. Dormancy stops automatic activity while the records and unfinished works remain in the archive.

Days when the program was not running are not filled with invented events. A fictional long wait does not have to stand between leaving and returning. Continuation begins only after an explicit restoration.

The story stops as you open the window again. The bookshop is still the one on North Street. The first note still has its awkward draft. The name that once sat empty has a history. This is what we want to entrust to time: a past you can continue from.

No fixed lifespan. Explicit dormancy preserves the complete archive and stops automatic activity, with explicit restoration available.

Give the everyday a place to stay.

01

Companionship

Start with a conversation. Share text, images and audio material, retaining receipt records and sources.

02

Journal

Put the days back on a calendar. Choose a date and read what was left there.

03

Memory

Find out why something was remembered. Return to the evidence, correct mistakes and allow views to change.

04

Life

Give an idea a next step. Keep goals, attempts, works and feedback connected to later action.

05

Settings

Choose appearance, language and how to converse. Preferences belong to the user; experiences remain in the life pack.

These mechanisms exist in the original development project; application source has not yet moved into this repository. Long-term continuity and autonomous growth remain under observation.

BUILD WITH US

Help this life find new possibilities.

A desktop pet, a group chat, a new voice, another body. SeedBank aims to give these experiments a shared foundation: interfaces and models may change while experiences keep their provenance. We welcome people building extensions, adapters and new places for an individual to live.

Integration mechanisms exist in the original R&D repository. This repository is still a private design draft; application source and the full development setup have not migrated yet. The protocol is under development, not a stable SDK commitment.

01

Chat platforms and tools

A QQ / OneBot adapter exists; explore Discord, Telegram and tool panels.

02

Model providers

stub, OpenAI-compatible, Anthropic and Codex adapters

New services and local models; better timeouts, cost reporting and failure handling.

03

Interfaces and bodies

Qt Quick / QML, local Live2D models, carrier bridge

New desktop, mobile or VR interfaces that preserve records and explicit choices.

04

Plugin infrastructure

Unified installation, permission manifests and hot reload are not implemented

Design manifests, capabilities, lifecycle and compatibility together.

The existing bridge exchanges newline-delimited JSON over stdin/stdout, using seedbank/bridge@1. A client sends this line to request the handshake. This is not a public HTTP endpoint:

{"id":"hello-1","op":"hello"}

There is room for Python, QML, frontend and model engineering, character production, accessibility and translation. The integration guide covers operations, request IDs, memory provenance, dormancy and artificial examples.

A little preparation for a first meeting.

  1. A place to live

    Name a new individual and choose where its life package lives on your computer. Existing archives can be continued.

  2. A way to talk

    Explore the interface with offline templates, then configure your own model service and budget if you wish.

  3. A look you like

    Appearance can be chosen separately. Obtain Live2D models from their authors and retain the required credits.

The first public version is planned as 0.0.1 Alpha. This repository is currently private and contains design and documentation only. No public installer is available.

A few things to know before you meet.

Will it remember everything forever?

Memory has sources, retrieval and revision mechanisms, but can still omit, misunderstand or fail to retrieve information. Original records give us a way to check and correct it. Long-term reliability needs real observation.

Where does my data live, and who receives it?

Life packages are stored locally. External model requests may include conversation and relevant memories; image understanding may also send the original image. Service charges may apply. Local storage does not make every feature offline.

What if I no longer want something brought up?

Letting go restricts future reference while preserving the history. It is not complete deletion from disk and cannot recall data already sent to an external service.

Is this already a complete digital life?

We are exploring how experiences can persist, how choices can have consequences and how an AI might develop a life of its own. Consciousness or a complete personhood has not been established. We cannot promise every individual will grow like the one in this story.
